Behind every song

Almost all of his songs are born from a true story. These are three that marked him.

🏥 The Hospital Where I Trained

In November 2026, a post about the hospital of Banes, Holguín — his hometown — showed its emergency ward in ruins. For Kiustin it was not just news: he trained as a physician at Banes' Nicaragua and Materno Infantil hospitals.

That same night the song was born. No mockery, no speeches: a son of the town singing to the hospital that made him a doctor. The video passed 11 thousand views and became the door through which thousands found his music.

🗺️ La Utopía Camina (The Walking Utopia)

The song that put him on the map: a rhythmic satire about the promised utopia and the reality of Cuban streets. Released in October 2026, it passed 150 thousand views on Facebook and gave Kiustin G his first big audience.

A whole family of songs was born from it — the controversy, the suitcase, the conga — and a line his audience repeats: the utopia walks… but the people walk further.

Read "La Utopía Responde" →

🕯️ La Cama Vacía (The Empty Bed)

His first song as Kiustin G, and the most sacred one. His mother, Clara Elena Rojas Rojas, died in Cuba on June 27, 2023, two months after he arrived in the United States. He lived her death from far away, like so many emigrants.

The night she died, she came to say goodbye to him in a dream — moments before the 4 a.m. call from his sister Melvis with the news. From that night comes the line that sums up his grief: "a mother never dies; she only learns to live inside you".

Later he turned the date itself into a song: "27 de Junio" (June 27), with real photos of his mother.
