PRACTICAL HEALTH · SYMPTOMS

🦵 Hand and leg cramps

A muscle cramp is a sudden involuntary contraction. Cramps often affect the legs but may also occur in the hands or other muscles.

📚 Medical sources

What should you notice?

  • Whether cramps occur during exercise, at rest or at night.
  • Sweating or possible fluid loss.
  • Frequency and severity.
  • Weakness, swelling, redness or skin changes.
  • New medicines or dose changes.

Possible causes

These are examples, not a diagnosis. The same symptom can have many different causes.

  • Muscle overuse.
  • Fluid loss.
  • Holding a position for a long time.
  • Some medicines.
  • Certain mineral, circulatory, neurologic or metabolic problems.

What can you do initially?

  • Stop the activity and gently stretch the affected muscle.
  • Replace fluids when appropriate.
  • Do not take high-dose mineral supplements simply because cramps occur without knowing the cause.
  • Seek evaluation for severe, frequent or persistent cramps.

🚨 Warning signs

  • Marked new muscle weakness.
  • Swelling, redness or significant skin changes.
  • Sudden weakness or numbness affecting one side of the body requires emergency evaluation.
  • Severe recurrent cramps associated with other significant symptoms.

If you believe the situation is life-threatening, call 911 in the United States.
