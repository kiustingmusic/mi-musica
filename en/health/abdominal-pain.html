PRACTICAL HEALTH · SYMPTOMS

🩻 Abdominal pain

Abdominal pain can be mild or severe, short-lived or persistent. Its location, pattern, duration and associated symptoms provide important clues.

📚 Medical sources

What should you notice?

  • Where the pain is located.
  • Whether it is sudden, constant, intermittent or worsening.
  • Fever, vomiting or diarrhea.
  • Blood in vomit or stool.
  • Abdominal swelling, tenderness or weight loss.

Possible causes

These are examples, not a diagnosis. The same symptom can have many different causes.

  • Gas or indigestion.
  • Gastrointestinal infections.
  • Constipation.
  • Muscle strain.
  • Many other digestive, urinary, reproductive or systemic conditions.

What can you do initially?

  • Drink fluids if tolerated and medically appropriate.
  • Observe the location and evolution of the pain.
  • Avoid taking laxatives or nonprescription pain medicines specifically for unexplained abdominal pain unless advised by a healthcare professional.
  • Seek assessment if pain worries you or persists.

🚨 Warning signs

  • Severe abdominal pain.
  • Pain associated with chest pressure or significant trauma.
  • Vomiting blood or black or bloody stool.
  • Marked abdominal swelling or severe tenderness.
  • Persistent vomiting, high fever or significant deterioration.

If you believe the situation is life-threatening, call 911 in the United States.
