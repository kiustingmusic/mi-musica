Pregnancy: understand, care and prevent

A clear guide to pregnancy development, prenatal care, ultrasound, prenatal diagnosis and symptoms that require medical evaluation.

🤰 Why is prenatal care important?

Pregnancy is a physiologic process, but every pregnancy is different. Prenatal care monitors maternal health and fetal development together.

Visits help confirm and accurately date the pregnancy, identify risk factors, monitor blood pressure, arrange appropriate testing and detect maternal, fetal or placental problems as early as possible.

Key idea: prenatal care is not only about confirming that the baby is growing. It also aims to anticipate complications and intervene when prevention or treatment may still make a difference.

📅 Pregnancy by trimester

First trimester · through 13+6 weeks Pregnancy confirmation and dating, fetal number, initial maternal assessment, prenatal screening and early fetal anatomy assessment when appropriate.
Second trimester Detailed fetal anatomy assessment, growth, placenta, amniotic fluid and follow-up of previously identified risk factors.
Third trimester Maternal health, fetal growth and wellbeing, fetal presentation and preparation for birth.

🩺 Early visits and laboratory testing

Early prenatal care generally includes a detailed medical and obstetric history, previous pregnancies, medical conditions, surgery, medications, allergies and relevant family history.

Maternal health Blood pressure, weight and other clinical measurements as appropriate.
Blood testing CBC, blood type and Rh status plus other clinically indicated tests.
Urine Urinalysis and urine culture can identify abnormalities and infections, including some that cause no symptoms.
Infections Pregnancy testing includes screening for selected infections such as HIV and hepatitis, with additional testing according to current guidance.

🩻 First-trimester ultrasound: more than seeing a heartbeat

Early ultrasound can confirm an intrauterine pregnancy, improve gestational dating, determine the number of fetuses and assess early pregnancy development.

In the late first trimester, particularly around 12 to 13+6 weeks, an appropriately performed specialized examination can provide substantially more information.

Early fetal anatomy assessment

Some major fetal structural abnormalities can already be identified at this stage. Not every anomaly is visible this early, but selected abnormalities may be recognized before the standard second-trimester anatomy examination.

🧠 Skull and central nervous system Early cranial formation and brain structures visible at this gestational age can be assessed.
❤️ Fetal heart Cardiac position and assessable anatomy are reviewed. Selected pregnancies may benefit from early fetal echocardiography.
👶 Face and profile The fetal profile and facial structures visible at this stage can be assessed.
🫁 Chest and abdomen Thoracic and abdominal structures and the abdominal wall are reviewed.
🦴 Spine and limbs The visible spine, presence of limbs and their general development can be assessed.
🫀 Placenta and cord Placental features, cord insertion and other relevant structures are documented when assessable.
Important: a normal first-trimester ultrasound cannot exclude every fetal anomaly. Some abnormalities develop or become visible later. Early anatomy assessment therefore does not replace the detailed second-trimester anatomy ultrasound.

🧬 Nuchal translucency and chromosome screening

Nuchal translucency is an ultrasound measurement of the fluid-filled space behind the fetal neck. It can form part of first-trimester screening strategies.

An increased nuchal translucency can be associated with a higher risk of chromosomal conditions and certain structural abnormalities, particularly congenital heart defects.

NIPT / cell-free DNA

Cell-free DNA screening (cfDNA/NIPT) is the most sensitive and specific screening test for the common fetal aneuploidies.

Screening is not diagnosis.
A low-risk NIPT result reduces the likelihood of selected chromosome conditions but does not guarantee that the fetus has no genetic condition or structural abnormality.

When screening is positive or an ultrasound abnormality is identified, genetic counseling and diagnostic testing such as chorionic villus sampling or amniocentesis may be offered depending on gestational age and the clinical situation.

🇪🇸 The Fetal Medicine Barcelona approach

Fetal Medicine Barcelona/BCNatal educational programs incorporate several concepts into first-trimester assessment that extend beyond simple pregnancy dating.

Early anatomy assessment Systematic evaluation of fetal anatomy and abnormalities potentially detectable during the first trimester.
Genetic sonogram Integration of ultrasound markers with prenatal screening.
Early fetal echocardiography Specialized early cardiac evaluation when clinically indicated.
Preeclampsia screening Early risk assessment using maternal information and, in some protocols, biophysical and biochemical markers.

This reflects the evolution of modern fetal medicine: the first trimester can provide information about fetal anatomy, genetics and maternal-placental risk.

🇺🇸 What do U.S. guidelines say?

AIUM, ACOG, SMFM, ACR and SRU have developed practice parameters for a detailed diagnostic obstetric ultrasound between 12+0 and 13+6 weeks.

This is a specialized, indication-driven examination, particularly relevant for pregnancies at increased risk of fetal or placental abnormalities and requiring advanced training for performance and interpretation.

ACOG also recommends that prenatal chromosome screening and diagnostic testing options be discussed and offered regardless of maternal age or baseline chromosome risk.

The second-trimester fetal anatomy examination remains essential and is ideally performed between 18 and 22 weeks.

🔬 The 18–22 week anatomy ultrasound

This is one of the major structural assessments of pregnancy and allows a more detailed evaluation of fetal anatomy.

Central nervous system Brain, intracranial structures and spine.
Face Profile and assessable facial structures.
Heart Cardiac anatomy and recommended views according to the examination.
Chest and abdomen Lungs, diaphragm, stomach, abdominal wall and other structures.
Kidneys and bladder Assessment of the fetal urinary system.
Limbs Assessment of long bones and visible extremities.

🌊 What is obstetric Doppler?

Doppler ultrasound assesses characteristics of blood flow. Depending on the clinical situation, vessels such as the uterine arteries, umbilical artery, middle cerebral artery and selected venous vessels may be evaluated.

It can be particularly useful in fetal growth disorders and selected maternal or placental conditions.

A Doppler examination does not automatically mean that a pregnancy is high risk. Its indication and interpretation depend on gestational age and the clinical situation.

🩸 Preeclampsia: assessing risk before disease develops

Preeclampsia is a pregnancy complication involving hypertension and potential dysfunction of multiple organ systems. Risk assessment begins during early prenatal care.

Some fetal-medicine programs use early screening algorithms combining maternal characteristics, blood pressure, uterine artery Doppler and selected biochemical markers.

In the United States, ACOG and SMFM recommend assessing clinical risk factors to identify patients who may benefit from low-dose aspirin.

Do not start aspirin on your own. When low-dose aspirin is indicated for preeclampsia prevention, the decision and treatment plan should be established with the clinician managing the pregnancy.

🧪 Important tests during pregnancy

Early pregnancy CBC, blood type and Rh status, urine testing and infection screening, with additional tests according to history and current guidance.
24–28 weeks Gestational diabetes screening is commonly performed during this period and may be performed earlier in selected higher-risk patients.
Later pregnancy Additional testing is performed according to pregnancy progression, medical history and applicable obstetric recommendations.

🥗 Nutrition and prenatal vitamins

A varied, balanced diet supports both maternal health and fetal development.

Folic acid Important for reducing the risk of neural tube defects. Individual requirements may differ in people with specific risk factors.
Iron Supports hemoglobin production and oxygen transport. Requirements increase during pregnancy.
Iodine Important for fetal neurologic development.
Hydration and food safety Adequate hydration and pregnancy-specific food-safety recommendations are important throughout pregnancy.

Supplement needs should be individualized. Taking a prenatal vitamin does not automatically make every additional supplement safe.

🚶 Physical activity

For uncomplicated pregnancies without contraindications, moderate physical activity is generally beneficial.

U.S. recommendations advise approximately 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity each week, adapted to individual fitness and obstetric circumstances.

💉 Vaccination during pregnancy

Maternal vaccination can protect the pregnant patient and provide antibodies to the baby during early infancy.

U.S. recommendations include pregnancy-specific guidance for vaccines such as influenza and Tdap. Maternal RSV vaccination may also be recommended within a specific gestational and seasonal window.

Vaccination recommendations can change and should be reviewed with a healthcare professional using current public-health guidance.

💊 Medications during pregnancy

Prescription drugs, over-the-counter medicines, supplements and herbal products do not all have the same safety profile during pregnancy.

Do not abruptly stop a necessary medication without medical advice. For some conditions, stopping treatment can create greater maternal or fetal risk than continuing an appropriately selected medication.

🚨 Urgent maternal warning signs

Seek prompt medical care for symptoms including:

  • Vaginal bleeding greater than spotting.
  • Fluid leaking from the vagina.
  • Severe abdominal pain that does not go away.
  • A severe headache that persists or worsens.
  • Significant vision changes.
  • Fever of 100.4 °F (38 °C) or higher.
  • Trouble breathing.
  • Chest pain or a significantly abnormal heartbeat.
  • Severe dizziness, fainting or seizures.
  • Severe vomiting with inability to keep fluids down.
  • Extreme swelling of the face or hands.
  • Severe swelling, redness or pain in an arm or leg.
  • The baby's usual movement stopping or becoming noticeably slower.

If symptoms are severe or there is immediate danger, seek emergency medical care.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Can fetal anomalies be detected in the first trimester?

Yes. Some major structural abnormalities can be identified during an early anatomy assessment performed by appropriately trained professionals. Other abnormalities become visible only later.

Does a normal 12–13 week ultrasound exclude all fetal abnormalities?

No. It provides valuable early information but does not replace the detailed second-trimester anatomy ultrasound.

Does a low-risk NIPT mean the baby has no medical condition?

No. NIPT is a screening test for selected chromosome abnormalities. It is not diagnostic and does not evaluate every genetic disorder or structural abnormality.

Does every pregnancy need Doppler studies?

No. Use depends on gestational age, history, ultrasound findings and the individual clinical situation.

Are natural supplements automatically safe during pregnancy?

No. Natural does not necessarily mean safe. Medications and supplements should be discussed with a healthcare professional.
