Prenatal care matters from the beginning

Prenatal care helps monitor maternal health and fetal development, identify risk factors and detect problems that may require additional evaluation or follow-up.

The frequency of visits and tests may vary according to gestational age, medical history, previous pregnancies and individual factors.

Pregnancy by trimester

First trimester Pregnancy confirmation and dating, initial evaluation, laboratory studies and risk assessment.
Second trimester Fetal growth follow-up and anatomical evaluation according to clinical indications.
Third trimester Monitoring of fetal growth, blood pressure, maternal-fetal wellbeing and preparation for birth.

What is commonly assessed?

Maternal health Blood pressure, symptoms, weight, medical history, medications and other relevant factors.
Fetal development Gestational age, growth, fetal heart rate and other parameters depending on pregnancy stage.
Laboratory testing Testing may include blood and urine studies, infection screening and gestational diabetes evaluation.
Prevention Nutrition, appropriate physical activity, medication safety, recommended vaccines and preparation for birth.

Ultrasound y Doppler

Ultrasound obstétrica

Ultrasound uses sound waves to create images of the pregnancy. It can help determine gestational age, assess fetal anatomy, evaluate the placenta and amniotic fluid and monitor growth.

Obstetric Doppler

Doppler can evaluate maternal, placental or fetal blood flow when clinically indicated.

Not every pregnancy requires the same Doppler studies.

Changes frecuentes durante el embarazo

  • Nausea.
  • Fatigue.
  • Breast tenderness.
  • More frequent urination.
  • Heartburn or constipation.
  • Mild low-back discomfort.

A common symptom should not be ignored when it is severe, begins suddenly or occurs with other concerning signs.

🚨 Warning signs

Seek medical attention if any of these symptoms occur:

  • Significant vaginal bleeding.
  • Leaking fluid.
  • Severe or persistent abdominal pain.
  • Severe headache or vision changes.
  • Chest pain or trouble breathing.
  • Fainting or seizures.
  • Severe vomiting with inability to keep fluids down.
  • Fever or feeling severely ill.
  • Marked swelling, pain or redness in one leg.
  • A marked decrease in fetal movement after a usual movement pattern has been established.

If symptoms are severe or there is immediate danger, seek emergency medical care.

Frequently asked questions

When should prenatal care begin?

After confirming pregnancy, contact a healthcare professional to arrange the first prenatal evaluation.

Does every pregnancy require Doppler?

No. Its use depends on the clinical situation and the assessment of the healthcare team.

Does a normal ultrasound guarantee everything will remain normal?

No. Ultrasound provides important information at the time of the examination but does not replace prenatal follow-up.

Can I take any medication?

No. Before starting, stopping or changing medications or supplements during pregnancy, consult a healthcare professional.
