PRACTICAL HEALTH · EMERGENCIES

Warning signs

Some symptoms may indicate a medical emergency. This guide can help you recognize situations in which you should not wait or try to determine the cause yourself.

🚨 If there is an immediate threat to life, call 911 in the United States. During a possible emergency, getting help quickly can be critical.

When should you act immediately?

❤️

Chest pain or pressure

Pay particular attention to pressure, squeezing, fullness or pain in the center of the chest, especially when it persists, returns or occurs with other symptoms.

  • Shortness of breath.
  • Cold sweat, nausea or lightheadedness.
  • Discomfort in the arms, back, neck, jaw or stomach.

→ For possible heart attack warning signs, call 911.

🧠

Sudden stroke signs

  • Sudden loss of balance or coordination.
  • Sudden vision changes.
  • Drooping or weakness on one side of the face.
  • Weakness or numbness of an arm or leg.
  • Sudden trouble speaking or understanding speech.
  • Sudden severe headache with no known cause.

→ Call 911 immediately, even if the symptoms go away.

🫁

Severe difficulty breathing

Severe breathing difficulty, choking or a person who is not breathing normally may represent an emergency.

→ Call 911 when breathing is severely compromised.

⚠️

Severe allergic reaction

A severe allergic reaction can begin very quickly.

  • Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat.
  • Difficulty breathing or swallowing.
  • Wheezing, fainting or a weak pulse.

→ Possible anaphylaxis requires emergency care: call 911.

👤

Unconsciousness or sudden confusion

Unconsciousness or a sudden unexplained change in mental status should be treated as a medical emergency.

→ Call 911 and do not leave the person alone.

🩸

Severe bleeding

Heavy or life-threatening bleeding requires immediate help.

→ Call 911 for severe bleeding or immediate danger.

🫀

Collapse and no normal breathing

A person who suddenly becomes unresponsive and is not breathing normally may be in cardiac arrest.

→ Call 911 immediately and follow the emergency dispatcher's instructions.

Important: this list does not include every possible emergency. If someone appears seriously ill, is rapidly getting worse, or you believe their life may be in danger, seek emergency help.
