Anemia: not every anemia is caused by iron deficiency

Educational guide to anemia, hemoglobin, iron, vitamin B12, blood loss, kidney disease, diagnosis and cause-specific treatment.

← Back to Health Center
Medical notice: Educational content. It does not replace individual medical consultation, diagnosis or treatment.

🩸 What is anemia?

Anemia is a condition in which the blood does not have enough healthy red blood cells or adequate hemoglobin to carry oxygen effectively to the body's tissues. Hemoglobin is an iron-containing protein inside red blood cells that helps move oxygen from the lungs throughout the body.

Not every anemia is caused by iron deficiency. Anemia can develop through several different mechanisms and medical conditions, so identifying the underlying cause is essential before deciding on treatment.

🔎 Main causes

Anemia can develop when the body does not produce enough red blood cells, when blood is lost, or when red blood cells are destroyed faster than they can be replaced.

Possible causes include iron deficiency, vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, blood loss, chronic kidney disease, inflammation, hemolysis, inherited disorders and diseases affecting the bone marrow.

🩺 Symptoms

Mild anemia may cause few or no symptoms. When anemia is more significant, symptoms may include fatigue, weakness, pale skin, dizziness, headache, shortness of breath, palpitations or reduced exercise tolerance.

These symptoms cannot identify the cause by themselves because they can also occur in many other medical conditions.

🧪 Complete blood count

A complete blood count, or CBC, is one of the main tests used to detect and begin evaluating anemia. It includes hemoglobin, hematocrit, red blood cell count and measurements related to the size and characteristics of red blood cells.

Mean corpuscular volume, or MCV, helps describe whether red blood cells are small, normal-sized or large, but it does not establish the cause by itself.

🧲 Iron and ferritin

Iron deficiency is common, but a low hemoglobin level does not automatically prove iron deficiency. Ferritin and other iron studies can help determine whether iron deficiency is actually present.

When iron-deficiency anemia is confirmed, it is also important to determine why iron deficiency developed, including possible blood loss, increased requirements or nutritional problems.

🧬 Vitamin B12, folate and other causes

Vitamin B12 and folate are important for normal blood cell production. Deficiency of either nutrient can cause anemia and requires a different evaluation from iron deficiency.

Anemia can also occur with chronic inflammation, increased red blood cell destruction, inherited blood disorders or bone marrow disease.

🩺 Anemia and chronic kidney disease

The kidneys help regulate red blood cell production through erythropoietin, a hormone that stimulates the bone marrow. In chronic kidney disease, reduced erythropoietin production can contribute to anemia.

💊 Treatment

There is no single treatment for anemia. Management depends on the type, severity and underlying cause. Documented iron deficiency may require iron replacement, while vitamin B12 or folate deficiency requires correction of the corresponding deficiency.

When anemia is associated with bleeding, kidney disease, inflammation, hemolysis or another medical condition, treating the underlying cause is an important part of management.

🍽️ Nutrition

A balanced diet can provide iron, vitamin B12, folate, protein and other nutrients needed for normal blood cell production. Dietary changes, however, do not replace medical investigation of established anemia.

👩‍⚕️ When to seek medical evaluation

Medical evaluation is appropriate for persistent unexplained fatigue, pale skin, shortness of breath with usual activity, recurrent palpitations, frequent dizziness or a laboratory result showing low hemoglobin.

🚨 When urgent care is needed

Chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, fainting, major bleeding, rapid deterioration or other severe symptoms require urgent medical attention. In the United States, call 911 for a medical emergency.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Is every anemia caused by iron deficiency?

No. Iron deficiency is common, but there are many other causes.

Does low hemoglobin mean I should take iron?

Not necessarily. Iron deficiency should first be established and the cause of anemia evaluated.

Can diet correct every type of anemia?

No. Nutrition is important, but treatment depends on the specific cause.
