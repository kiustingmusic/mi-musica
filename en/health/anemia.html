Anemia: not every anemia is caused by iron deficiency

Educational guide to anemia, hemoglobin, iron, vitamin B12, blood loss, kidney disease, diagnosis and cause-specific treatment.

← Back to Health Center
Medical notice: Educational content. It does not replace individual medical consultation, diagnosis or treatment.

🩸 What is anemia?

Anemia develops when the blood has a lower-than-normal amount of healthy red blood cells or adequate hemoglobin to carry oxygen effectively.

🔎 Symptoms

Anemia can cause fatigue, weakness, paleness, shortness of breath, dizziness, headache or palpitations. Mild anemia may cause no symptoms.

🧪 Complete blood count

A CBC evaluates hemoglobin, hematocrit, red blood cell count and measures such as mean corpuscular volume, which can help determine the cause.

🧲 Iron and ferritin

Iron deficiency is common, but it is not the only cause of anemia. Ferritin and other iron studies help determine whether iron deficiency is actually present.

🧬 Other causes

Anemia may also result from vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, blood loss, kidney disease, chronic inflammation, hemolysis, bone marrow disorders or inherited conditions.

💊 Cause-specific treatment

Treatment depends on the type and cause. Not every anemia requires iron. Automatically treating anemia as iron deficiency can delay identification of the real cause.

🍽️ Nutrition

A balanced diet provides iron, vitamin B12, folate and protein, but established anemia may require investigation and specific treatment in addition to dietary changes.

🚨 When urgent care is needed

Severe shortness of breath, chest pain, fainting, major bleeding, very rapid heartbeat or marked deterioration requires urgent medical care.
